## 1. What is ScrumReady?

ScrumReady is a Scrum Master certification exam prep platform built for candidates pursuing the **CSM** (Certified ScrumMaster) from Scrum Alliance and the **PSM I** (Professional Scrum Master I) from Scrum.org. It combines a guided learning environment with a realistic exam simulator so that candidates can build foundational knowledge, stress-test it under timed conditions, and understand exactly what they got wrong — all in one place.

All question content is aligned with the **Scrum Guide 2020**.

### Who it is for

- Professionals preparing to sit the CSM or PSM I exam for the first time
- Re-takers who want to identify and close specific knowledge gaps
- Practitioners who want to reinforce Scrum fundamentals through deliberate practice

### Tagline and brand positioning

**Sprint toward certification.** ScrumReady treats exam prep the way Scrum treats product delivery: short, focused sessions with clear feedback, continuous improvement, and a concrete definition of "done" — passing your exam.

### The two-mode philosophy

ScrumReady is built around a single governing principle:

> **Quiz mode teaches. Simulator mode tests. Results mode coaches.**

Every design decision follows from this rule. Quiz Mode is energetic, explanatory, and encouraging. The Exam Simulator is calm, focused, and exam-faithful. The results screen brings both together by surfacing what you know, what you missed, and why.

## 2. Certifications Covered

ScrumReady supports exam simulation and question content for both major Scrum Master credentials.

| Certification | Issuing Body | Questions | Time Limit | Passing Score |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CSM — Certified ScrumMaster | Scrum Alliance | 50 (random) | 60 minutes | 74% |
| PSM I — Professional Scrum Master I | Scrum.org | 80 (random) | 60 minutes | 85% |

### CSM (Certified ScrumMaster)

Offered by Scrum Alliance, CSM is typically taken after completing a two-day in-person or virtual course with a Certified Scrum Trainer. The exam consists of 50 questions, and candidates must score at least 74% to pass. The certification validates core Scrum competency and is widely recognized across industries.

### PSM I (Professional Scrum Master I)

Offered by Scrum.org, PSM I is a globally recognized credential that validates a candidate's understanding of the Scrum framework as defined in the Scrum Guide. The exam is challenging — 80 questions in 60 minutes with an 85% passing threshold — and covers Scrum theory, the Scrum Team, Events, Artifacts, and scenario-based application. No training course is required to sit the exam.

Both certifications test overlapping Scrum knowledge, so a candidate preparing for one will find the ScrumReady content directly applicable to the other.

## 3. Modes of Practice

ScrumReady offers two distinct practice modes, each serving a different phase of exam preparation.

### Quiz Mode — Agile Launchpad

Quiz Mode is the learning-first environment. Questions are delivered one at a time, and after every submission you receive immediate feedback: whether you answered correctly, the correct answer highlighted in context, and a rationale card that explains the underlying Scrum concept. The interface is designed to feel like an Agile training session — progress-oriented, encouraging, and informative.

**Use Quiz Mode when** you are building or reinforcing your Scrum knowledge, you want to understand the *why* behind each answer, or you have scored below 85% on a simulator and want to close specific gaps.

### Exam Simulator — Quiet Sprint

The Exam Simulator is the pressure-test environment. It mirrors the real exam experience: a countdown timer, free navigation between questions, the ability to mark questions for review, and — critically — no correctness feedback until the exam is submitted. The interface is intentionally minimal so that nothing distracts from the question in front of you.

**Use the Exam Simulator when** you believe you have a solid grasp of the material and want to test whether that knowledge holds under timed, exam-like conditions.

### How the two modes complement each other

The recommended path is sequential: build understanding in Quiz Mode, validate it in the Simulator. A high Readiness Score in Quiz Mode (85%+) signals you are ready to stress-test that knowledge in a full simulation. A simulation result that reveals specific weak areas sends you back to the relevant Quiz Mode banks to close those gaps.

## 4. Quiz Mode — Feature Details

### Question banks

Quiz Mode is organized into six thematic banks, each targeting a specific area of the Scrum curriculum.

| Bank | Topic | Status |
|---|---|---|
| QB 00 | Deep Dive: Scrum & Agile (Agile Manifesto) | Free |
| QB 01 | Deep Dive: Scrum Theory & Framework | Free |
| QB 02 | Deep Dive: The Scrum Team | Free |
| QB 03 | Deep Dive: Scrum Events | Premium (coming soon) |
| QB 04 | Deep Dive: Artifacts & Commitments | Premium (coming soon) |
| QB 05 | Scrum Advanced Scenarios | Premium (coming soon) |

Each bank contains **40 questions**. At the start of every session, ScrumReady randomly selects **20 questions** from the pool, so no two sessions are identical. This prevents memorization of question order and keeps repeated practice meaningful.

All questions are aligned with the **Scrum Guide 2020**.

### Instant feedback

After you submit an answer, the interface immediately reveals the result. Correct answers are highlighted in green. If you selected an incorrect option, it is highlighted in red while the correct answer is simultaneously revealed, so you can see both your mistake and the right choice in the same view.

### Rationale explanations

Below every answer reveal is a rationale card explaining the reasoning. The card uses two styles depending on your result:

- **Key Scrum idea** — shown when you answer correctly. Reinforces the concept so the principle sticks, not just the answer.
- **Watch out for this trap** — shown when you answer incorrectly. Identifies the specific misconception or distractor that makes that question difficult.

This is where most of the learning happens. Reading the rationale even on questions you got right is strongly recommended.

### Explanation feedback

At the bottom of every rationale card, a small prompt asks: **"Was this explanation helpful?"** You can respond with **Yes** or **No** in one tap. If the explanation was unclear or confusing, selecting No opens a short optional text field where you can describe what was missing. This feedback is used to improve explanations over time. Your response is saved to your account and does not affect your score.

### Question types

Quiz banks include three question types:

| Type | Behavior |
|---|---|
| Single Select | Choose exactly one correct answer (radio button) |
| Multiple Select | Choose all correct answers (checkboxes); all must be correct to score the point |
| True / False | Select True or False |

Question type is labeled clearly at the top of each question card before you answer.

### Streak tracking

ScrumReady tracks consecutive correct answers during a session. When you build a streak of two or more, a streak indicator appears below the progress bar ("3 in a row — keep it up!"). A single incorrect answer resets the streak. This gives you a real-time signal about whether your understanding is consistent or intermittent.

### Sprint Progress indicator

A circular progress ring in the top-left of each question shows your position within the session (e.g., Question 7 of 20). A horizontal progress bar fills as you advance. The combination gives you both a precise count and a visual sense of how far through the session you are.

### Session score and Readiness Score

When you complete all 20 questions, the sprint ends and a results screen appears. It displays:

- A **Readiness Score** percentage (score ring, color-coded: green at 85%+, amber at 70–84%, red below 70%)
- Your raw score (correct / total)
- A pass/fail readiness indicator with motivational copy
- Navigation to try again or return to Sprint Hub

The 85% threshold used for the quiz Readiness Score mirrors the PSM I passing requirement, giving you a concrete benchmark to aim for before moving to the Simulator.

### Attempt saving and history

Each completed quiz session is automatically saved to your account. Your session history is accessible from the My Exam Results page, where you can track your improvement across multiple attempts of the same bank.

## 5. Exam Simulator — Feature Details

### Simulation banks

The Exam Simulator contains three simulation banks, each targeting a different depth of Scrum knowledge.

| Bank | Name | Difficulty | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| SIM 01 | Simulation 1 — Baseline | Foundational | Free |
| SIM 02 | Simulation 2 — Refinement | Applied / Scenario | Premium (coming soon) |
| SIM 03 | Simulation 3 — Advanced Scrum Mastery | Expert / Edge Cases | Premium (coming soon) |

Each bank contains a pool of **100 questions**. See [Section 9](#9-difficulty-progression-simulation-banks) for details on what distinguishes each level.

### Exam type selection

Before starting a simulation, you choose which exam you are preparing for — **CSM** or **PSM I**. This selection determines how many questions are drawn and what the passing threshold will be for your results:

| Exam Type | Questions Drawn | Time | Passing Score |
|---|---|---|---|
| CSM | 50 of 100 (random) | 60 minutes | 74% |
| PSM I | 80 of 100 (random) | 60 minutes | 85% |

After selecting your exam type, a start screen shows a confirmation summary (question count, time limit, passing threshold) before the timer begins.

### Countdown timer

The timer counts down from the moment you start the simulation. As time decreases, the timer changes color to signal urgency:

| Time Remaining | Timer Color | Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| 10 minutes or more | Navy (neutral) | No change |
| Under 10 minutes | Amber | Warning state |
| Under 3 minutes | Red | Critical, animated pulse |

If the timer reaches zero, the exam is automatically submitted with whatever answers you have recorded.

### Free navigation

Unlike Quiz Mode, you are not locked into a linear sequence. During the simulator you can move freely between any question using the Previous and Next buttons, or by clicking directly on any question number in the Navigator panel.

### Question Navigator panel

A grid panel on the right side of the screen shows every question number in the exam. Each cell is color-coded:

| Color | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Dark navy | Answered |
| Amber | Marked for Review |
| Light grey | Unanswered |
| Blue ring | Currently viewing |

You can click any cell in the Navigator to jump directly to that question. This is especially useful in the final minutes when you want to quickly visit unanswered or marked questions.

### Mark for Review

Any question can be flagged with "Mark for Review" using the button in the upper-right corner of the question card. Marked questions appear in amber in the Navigator panel. This lets you move forward without losing track of questions you want to revisit before submitting.

### No feedback during exam

During an active simulation, no correctness information is shown. Selecting an answer highlights your selection in blue — no green, no red, no rationale. This is intentional: the Exam Simulator is designed to replicate the actual exam experience, where feedback is withheld until results are revealed.

### Submission

When you are ready to end the exam, click "Finish Exam." If you have unanswered questions, a confirmation dialog warns you how many questions will be marked incorrect if you proceed. You can return to the exam or submit anyway.

### Results screen

After submission, a results screen displays:

- A **score ring** showing your percentage (color-coded green, amber, or red relative to the passing threshold for your selected exam type)
- A **pass/fail indicator** (PASSED or NOT YET) with your raw score
- The passing threshold for context

From the results screen you can navigate to the full answer review, view your history, or return to Sprint Hub.

### Full answer review mode

After completing a simulation, you can enter review mode to go through every question with full answer visibility. The review interface includes:

- **All / Correct / Incorrect tabs** to filter which questions you see
- For each question: your selected answer, the correct answer, and the full rationale explanation
- A **Navigator panel** showing the full question grid (green = correct, red = incorrect)
- Click any cell in the Navigator to jump directly to that question
- **Explanation feedback** — the same Yes / No feedback prompt appears below each rationale so you can flag unclear explanations while they are fresh

The review mode is where the Simulator becomes a teaching tool. Spending time here — especially on the Incorrect tab — is the most direct path to improving your next score.

### Attempt saving and history

Each completed simulation is automatically saved to your account, including your exam type selection. You can review any past attempt from the History page.

## 6. Sprint Hub (Dashboard)

Sprint Hub is the central navigation page for ScrumReady. It is your starting point for every practice session.

**How to reach it:** From any page, click "Sprint Hub" in the top navigation. You can also navigate directly to `/dashboard`.

### Mode selection

The top of Sprint Hub shows two cards: **Quiz Mode** and **Exam Simulator**. Clicking a card switches the dashboard to display the relevant banks and stats for that mode. The active card is highlighted.

You can also arrive at Sprint Hub with a mode pre-selected by using the landing page buttons:
- "Start Quiz Practice →" opens Sprint Hub in Quiz Mode
- "Launch Exam Simulator →" opens Sprint Hub in Simulator Mode

### Stats bar

Below the mode cards, a stats bar shows your aggregate performance for the active mode. A **"My Exam Results →"** link at the top right of the bar lets you jump directly to your full attempt history. Stats update automatically as you complete sessions.

**Quiz Mode stats:**

| Stat | Definition |
|---|---|
| Sessions | Total number of completed quiz sessions |
| Questions | Total questions answered across all sessions |
| Accuracy | Overall percentage of questions answered correctly |

**Exam Simulator stats:**

| Stat | Definition |
|---|---|
| Attempts | Total number of completed simulations |
| Best Score | Highest percentage score across all simulation attempts |
| Avg Score | Average percentage score across all simulation attempts |

### Bank cards

Below the stats bar, bank cards list the available question banks for the active mode. Each card shows the bank name, question count details, and time estimate. Clicking a card launches that bank.

### I passed my certification!

A green **"I passed my certification!"** button appears on Sprint Hub (and on the landing page). Clicking it opens a modal where you can share your real-world certification success. See [Section 10](#10-certification-pass--testimonials) for details.

## 7. My Exam Results

The My Exam Results page (`/history`) provides a full chronological log of every completed attempt — both quiz sessions and simulations.

**What is tracked for each attempt:**
- Bank name and exam type (PSM I or CSM, for simulations)
- Date and time of completion
- Score (correct / total)
- Percentage score, color-coded against the passing threshold

**Summary stats at the top** show your total attempts, best score, and average score across all recorded history.

Clicking any attempt in the list opens the full answer review for that session, so you can revisit a past simulation's questions and rationale at any time. The explanation feedback prompt is also available in the history review, so you can flag unclear rationale even on older attempts.

History is per-account and persists between sessions. Sign in to the same account on any device to access your full history.

## 8. Randomization

### Why questions are randomized

Randomization is a deliberate design choice, not a technical detail. If you see the same 20 questions in the same order every time you open a bank, you will memorize the pattern rather than learn the concepts. Randomization ensures that every session tests genuine understanding.

### How it works

| Mode | Pool Size | Draw Size | When Drawn |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quiz Mode | 40 questions per bank | 20 per session | On session start |
| Simulator (CSM) | 100 main + 10 Agile Manifesto | 48 main + 2 Agile Manifesto = 50 | On exam type selection |
| Simulator (PSM I) | 100 questions per bank | 80 per attempt | On exam type selection |

For CSM simulations, 2 Agile Manifesto questions (4% of 50, matching the CSM ECO Domain A weighting) are drawn from a dedicated supplement bank and mixed into the 50-question set.

The draw is a full random shuffle of the pool, then a slice to the target count. No question is repeated within a single session or simulation.

### Benefit for retakers

Because the draw is random each time, retaking the same bank always yields a partially different set of questions. Over multiple sessions you will encounter the full pool. This makes repetition productive — each session covers different material from the same bank, rather than drilling the same subset.

## 9. Difficulty Progression (Simulation Banks)

The three simulation banks are sequenced deliberately. Starting at Baseline and progressing to Advanced Scrum Mastery is the recommended path.

### Simulation 1 — Baseline (Free)

Baseline focuses on **direct recall from the Scrum Guide**. Questions test whether you know the rules: accountabilities, events, artifacts, time-boxes, commitments, and the five Scrum values. Distractors tend to be plausible but clearly outside the Scrum Guide. If you struggle here, go back to Quiz Mode banks QB 01 and QB 02 before attempting Refinement.

**Good indicator you are ready to move on:** Scoring 80%+ consistently on Baseline.

### Simulation 2 — Refinement (Premium, coming soon)

Refinement introduces **applied and scenario-based questions**. Rather than asking "what is the Sprint Review?", it presents a situation — a team behaving in a particular way — and asks whether that behavior reflects good Scrum practice, and why. These questions require you to apply Scrum principles, not just recall definitions.

**Good indicator you are ready to move on:** Scoring 80%+ consistently on Refinement.

### Simulation 3 — Advanced Scrum Mastery (Premium, coming soon)

Advanced Scrum Mastery targets **expert-level reasoning**: coaching scenarios, organizational change, scaled Scrum edge cases, and situations where multiple answers seem defensible. These questions are designed to challenge candidates who have already passed Baseline and Refinement comfortably, and reflect the difficulty ceiling of the PSM I exam.

### Recommended progression path

```
Quiz Banks (QB 01, QB 02)
    → Simulation Baseline (SIM 01)
        → Quiz Banks (QB 03, QB 04, QB 05)
            → Simulation Refinement (SIM 02)
                → Simulation Advanced (SIM 03)
                    → Schedule your exam
```

Move to the next level when you are scoring at or above the PSM I passing threshold (85%) on the current level consistently. Attempting Advanced Scrum Mastery before you are comfortable with Baseline is likely to erode confidence rather than build it.

## 12. Getting Started

Follow these steps to go from zero to your first practice session.

### Step 1 — Create your account

Go to [scrumready.aisolutionmaven.com](https://scrumready.aisolutionmaven.com) and click **Get started** or **Create your free account**. Sign up with your email address and a password. No credit card is required to access free content. The landing page hero shows a **"Free — no credit card required"** badge so new visitors know immediately that sign-up is free.

If you already have an account, click **Sign in**.

### Step 2 — Navigate to Sprint Hub

After signing in you will be on the landing page. Click **Start Quiz Practice** or **Launch Exam Simulator** to go to Sprint Hub with the relevant mode pre-selected. You can also click **Sprint Hub** in the top navigation at any time. On mobile, the navigation links collapse into a hamburger menu (☰) in the top-right corner.

### Step 3 — Choose your mode

On Sprint Hub, select **Quiz Mode** if you are still building your Scrum knowledge, or **Exam Simulator** if you want to test yourself under timed conditions.

**Not sure which to start with?** Start with Quiz Mode. Even experienced practitioners benefit from the rationale explanations, and it is a faster way to identify gaps than a full 80-question simulation.

### Step 4 — Select a bank and start

Click any bank card to launch that session. For first-time users:

- **Quiz Mode:** Start with QB 01 (Theory & Framework), then QB 02 (The Scrum Team).
- **Exam Simulator:** Start with SIM 01 (Baseline). Select your target certification (**CSM** or PSM I — CSM is the default) on the exam type screen, review your settings, and click Start Simulation.

### Step 5 — Review your results

When a session ends, spend time on the results screen. In Quiz Mode, re-read the rationale for every question — especially the ones you got right, since the "Key Scrum idea" reinforces the principle behind the answer. In the Simulator, use the Incorrect tab in the review mode to work through every question you missed.

### Step 6 — Progress to the Simulator

When you are consistently scoring **85% or higher** in Quiz Mode across multiple sessions of the same bank, you are ready to run a full simulation. The 85% target mirrors the PSM I passing threshold, giving you a concrete readiness benchmark.

Use your simulation results to decide where to focus next. A strong Baseline score with a weak showing on events or artifacts should send you back to QB 03 or QB 04 before your next simulation.

### Step 7 — Track your progress

Visit **My Exam Results** (in the top navigation, or via the link on your Sprint Hub stats bar) to review past attempts and monitor your improvement over time. Watching your scores improve across repeated attempts is the most direct evidence of exam readiness.

### Step 8 — Share your success

When you pass your real exam, come back and click **"I passed my certification!"** on Sprint Hub or the landing page. Share your story — it takes less than a minute and helps other candidates know what to expect.
